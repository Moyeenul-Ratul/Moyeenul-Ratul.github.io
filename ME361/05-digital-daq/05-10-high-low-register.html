HIGH and LOW: how a word travels on wires

8-bit registerHIGH (1)LOW (0)

Electronics represents a bit by a voltage. In 5 V TTL (transistor–transistor logic) a HIGH of about 5 V means 1 and a LOW of about 0 V means 0. A receiver accepts anything from 2 to 5.5 V as HIGH and anything below about 0.8 V as LOW. The undefined gap in between is a noise margin: a half-volt spike on a LOW line still reads LOW.

Parallel transmission uses one wire per bit, so the whole word arrives at once. Serial transmission uses one wire and sends the bits one after another, each held for a fixed time \(\delta t\), so a word takes \(M\,\delta t\).

Parallel is an eight-lane bridge: a whole word crosses side by side. Serial is a single-lane bridge: the bits queue up, but you only have to build one lane.

Press Load 10110011: unsigned 179, two's complement −77. Press Negate: 01001101 = +77. Now set 01111111 (+127) and press Add 1. The pattern becomes 10000000, which two's complement reads as −128: integer overflow wraps round, it does not stop at the top.
