Start at 4 bits: the staircase has 16 levels and the error is a sawtooth inside the blue ±Q/2 band. Raise M to 8 and 12 and watch Q and the error shrink by 2 for every bit. Then set M = 8 and shrink the amplitude to 0.05 V: the sine turns into two or three steps and the relative error reaches tens of percent. Finally push the amplitude past 5 V with mean 5 V: the red bands show saturation, where the output flattens.

A 0–10 V converter with a ½-LSB bias reads a 100 mV and a 1 V signal. Find the relative quantization error \(e_Q/E_i\) for 8 and 12 bits.

  1. \(Q_8=10/256=39.1\) mV and \(Q_{12}=10/4096=2.44\) mV.
  2. \(e_Q=\tfrac12Q\): 19.5 mV (8-bit) and 1.22 mV (12-bit).
  3. 100 mV signal: 19.5 % (8-bit), 1.2 % (12-bit).
  4. 1 V signal: 1.95 % (8-bit), 0.12 % (12-bit).

What this means: the same absolute error hurts a small signal ten times more. Amplify first.
