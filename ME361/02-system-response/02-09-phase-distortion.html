Worked example 1

Same two sine waves, two delays: one keeps its shape, one does not

Phase-distortion labu(t) = sin t + sin 5tmeasured signal

The sin t part is always delayed by 0.35 rad (0.35 s). Set the 5t lag to 1.75 rad: both parts are 0.35 s late and the orange curve is the grey one shifted right. Now try 5.00 rad (system 2 of the problem) and 0.35 rad: same amplitudes, different shape.

A signal \(u(t)=\sin t+\sin5t\) (t in s) is measured by two systems. The first records \(v(t)=\sin(t-0.35)+\sin(5t-1.75)\); the second records \(w(t)=\sin(t-0.35)+\sin(5t-5)\). Neither changes an amplitude. Which record is distorted?

  1. Delay of each component \(=\) phase lag ÷ angular frequency.
  2. System 1: \(0.35/1=0.35\) s and \(1.75/5=0.35\) s. Equal. Phase lag is proportional to frequency (0.35 rad per rad/s).
  3. So \(v(t)=u(t-0.35)\): an exact copy, 0.35 s late.
  4. System 2: \(0.35/1=0.35\) s but \(5/5=1.00\) s. The fast component arrives 0.65 s later than the slow one.
  5. Meaning: \(w(t)\) is distorted even though both amplitudes are perfect. Peaks appear where \(u\) had none. Phase alone can ruin a waveform.
