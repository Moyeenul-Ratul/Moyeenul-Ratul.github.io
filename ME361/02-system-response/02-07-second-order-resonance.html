Below, at and above resonance: where an instrument is honest

Frequency responseyour ζζ = 0.7

Set ζ = 0.2 and slide the frequency to the peak: about 2.5 times too big. At r = 1 the phase is −90°. Raise ζ past 0.71 and the peak disappears. The dashed ζ = 0.7 curve stays inside the green ±5 % band up to r ≈ 0.6.

Where is \(M\) largest? Minimise the denominator: \(\frac{d}{dr}\big[(1-r^2)^2+4\zeta^2r^2\big]=-4r(1-r^2)+8\zeta^2r=0\) gives \(r^2=1-2\zeta^2\).

Resonance frequency and peak $$\omega_R=\omega_n\sqrt{1-2\zeta^2},\qquad M_{\max}=\frac{1}{2\zeta\sqrt{1-\zeta^2}}$$

A peak exists only if \(1-2\zeta^2>0\), i.e. \(\zeta<0.707\). The resonance frequency \(\omega_R\) is where a steady sine is most amplified; it is lower than \(\omega_d\), which is free ringing.

The transmission band is the low-frequency range where \(M\approx1\): measure here. The resonance band is around \(\omega_R\), where \(M>1\). The filter band is at high frequency, where \(M\) is below 0.707 (−3 dB) and the signal is attenuated.
