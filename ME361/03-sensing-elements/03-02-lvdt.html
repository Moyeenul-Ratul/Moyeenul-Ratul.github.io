The left plot is the LVDT’s characteristic. The black curve is the magnitude of the output, the V-graph a meter would show. The blue curve is what a phase-sensitive demodulator delivers: the same magnitude with a sign, a straight line through zero.

The right plot shows the two waveforms in time. The output has the excitation’s frequency; only its size and its phase carry the information.

Move x from −6 to +6 mm. Inside about ±2 mm the curves are straight (green band). Beyond, the core leaves the coils and the output peaks and falls. Watch the output trace flip from in phase to opposite phase as x crosses zero. Then raise the coil mismatch: the bottom of the V rounds off and never reaches zero. That residual is the null voltage, and it limits the smallest displacement you can resolve near the centre.

An LVDT reads 0.30 V both at x = +0.5 mm and at x = −0.5 mm. How does the instrument tell them apart?
