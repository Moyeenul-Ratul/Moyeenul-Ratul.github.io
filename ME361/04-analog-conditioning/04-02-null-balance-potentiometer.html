Measuring voltage · null balance

The potentiometer weighs an unknown voltage against a known one

The slide wire is a voltage divider: a uniform resistor whose voltage drop is proportional to length, so the voltage between B and the slider A is \(E_{AB}=(R_x/R_T)\,E_i=x\,E_i\). When G reads zero, no current flows through the unknown source, and

Potentiometer at balance$$E_m=E_{AB}=\frac{R_x}{R_T}\,E_i$$

The supply \(E_i\) is first set against a standard cell of known emf. Good instruments reach uncertainties below 1 µV, which is why they once calibrated thermocouples. Digital voltmeters replaced them by using a very high input resistance instead of a null.

Deflection vs nulltrue source: 1.000 V behind Rs

Deflection: with Rs = 10 kΩ and Rm = 40 kΩ the meter reads 0.800 V. Switch to null, wire supply 2.000 V, and move x until G sits at zero: x = 0.5000 reads 1.000 V for any Rs. With Rs = 1 MΩ the needle barely moves near balance, so finding the null gets harder.
