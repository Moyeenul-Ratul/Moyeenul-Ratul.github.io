All waves have amplitude 1; the coefficients follow from the same Euler formulas. Jumps (square, sawtooth) give coefficients falling as \(1/n\): slow convergence. Mere corners (triangle, rectified sine) give \(1/n^2\): a few terms suffice. A smoother signal has a narrower spectrum.

The Gibbs phenomenon: next to a jump every partial sum overshoots by about 9 % of the jump. More terms squeeze the overshoot closer to the jump but never remove it.

Square wave: step from 1 to 5 terms and count the humps per half-cycle (one per term). Push to 40: the RMS error falls, but the peak stays near 1.18, 9 % of the jump of 2. The triangle looks right with 3 terms. The rectified sine has DC plus even multiples of the input frequency only.
